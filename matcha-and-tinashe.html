Why are matcha and Tinashe having a comeback?

One is a centuries-old green tea. The other is a pop artist. So why do they both feel like they're having a moment again? And what can their cultural trajectories tell us about the way things become desirable?

FIG. 001 — TWO VERY DIFFERENT THINGS. ONE VERY INTERESTING QUESTION.

At some point, the internet decided that matcha was a personality. Then, somewhere in the background, Tinashe started getting talked about again. Suddenly, two things that had been around for a while seemed to be occupying a new kind of cultural space.

Which got me thinking: what actually makes something come back? Is it nostalgia? Timing? A new audience discovering something for the first time? Or does culture simply need a little distance before it can recognise what it had in front of it all along?

Maybe a comeback isn't always about becoming relevant again. Maybe it's about being seen differently.
